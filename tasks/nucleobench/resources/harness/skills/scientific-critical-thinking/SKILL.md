# Critical Evaluation During Optimization

Use proportionate scrutiny to make the next research decision, not to produce
a review report every turn. Missing evidence is an open question, not evidence
of harm. A candidate may test a plausible hypothesis before its mechanism is
established; state the uncertainty and choose a useful comparison. Distinguish
the following evidence:

- A campaign measurement is an observed evaluator outcome for one exact sequence.
- A research annotation records a pre-evaluation hypothesis, not a proven cause.
- A motif score or fitted prediction is a local proxy, not a new measurement.
- Literature supports mechanisms in its stated biological and assay context.
  Transfer to the campaign objective remains a hypothesis to test.

## Interpret Feedback

Retrieve the relevant measured candidates and their original rationales.
For a claimed effect, check what actually changed in the reconstructed sequences,
what remained fixed, and which other explanation could account for the result.
Adjust confidence to the comparison, not just to the winning score.

Repeated high scores from one family support a local direction, not global
optimality. A weak candidate may reflect its construction, motif context, or
background rather than failure of the entire proposed program. Large sequence
distance is not proof of a different mechanism. Keep credible alternatives
with an explicit comparison that would strengthen or weaken each explanation.

Identify selection effects: the measured panel is adaptively chosen and is not
a random sample of sequence space. A higher batch average can result from
sampling the same good program more densely. Multiple submissions or annotations
for one measured sequence are one observation, not independent corroboration.
Lack of selection is not a negative assay result; previously submitted but
unmeasured candidates remain eligible under the turn's novelty contract.

## Research Sources

Search to answer a concrete unresolved question or challenge the current
explanation. Prefer primary experiments and inspect the source, cell context,
assay, controls, and measured endpoint. A search snippet is a lead, not evidence
for every claim in its title. Record the source URL or DOI and the claim it
actually supports in research notes. Distinguish missing access from negative
evidence; do not fill an inaccessible paper with guessed findings.

Use the existing structured web tools for search and retrieval and Context7
for software documentation. No additional search account, API credential, or
paid diagram service is required by this skill.

## Make a Decision

Retain a concise record of the leading explanation, the strongest unresolved
rival, supporting and contradicting measured IDs, and the next useful test.
Update this record when feedback changes the evidence, including negative
results. Do not rewrite original submission rationales after seeing outcomes.

Prefer a calculation or controlled comparison that can change the allocation
over a longer narrative. Concentration is reasonable when comparative evidence
supports it; uncertainty about sparsely sampled alternatives is not evidence
that they are inferior. Keep claims limited to the measured objective and
tested contexts. This is an adaptive benchmark search, not a clinical evidence
grading or manuscript peer-review workflow.
